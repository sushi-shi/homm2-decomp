# C07: tagged network payload views

Applied real signed startup fields and explicit tag-specific record views.
Both transports keep the byte payload erased until the dispatch arm selects
its layout; neither treats guest information as a startup record. Winsock's
three offsetof-based accesses now use the actual packed record members.

This is a structural recovery, not a cast-count target. Four redundant
startup-store casts are removed; the explicit record views add five casts,
for one net additional written cast. The remaining void-to-record conversions
express the wire boundary. They do not establish portable C++ object lifetime
or validate a packet's length.

## Owners and complete consumer review

DirectPlayStartupMessage and WinsockStartupMessage are separate packed26-byte
protocol records: two signed byte fields followed by six32-bit player IDs or
addresses at offset2. Both receiving functions use retail MOVSX loads for the
first two fields. Accordingly DirectPlay's former plain char fields and
Winsock's former u8 fields become i8. The only other named record consumers
are dpnet_init and wsnet_init; their byte stores and complete26-byte sends
remain unchanged. The destination fields now supply the same narrowing without
an intervening u8 cast. There is no new clamp or unsigned reinterpretation.

dpEvaluateMessage's carrier remains named startup but is now void*. Its guest
arm converts directly to SNetPlayerInfo, and its startup arm explicitly selects
DirectPlayStartupMessage. Renaming the carrier to payload was measured and
changed frame operands despite preserving total size; it is not retained.
wsEvaluateMessage's message carrier is likewise void*, with direct views for
the two guest operations and three startup members. A false typed carrier is
not introduced merely to save repeated casts.

SNetPlayerInfo remains the real34-byte guest record, including its preserved
compression and reserved fields. Winsock's comparison of the received record
address against a table-element address is unchanged: retail compares addresses,
not record contents. The normal guest record and receive buffer have different
owners; replacing this with a content comparison would change behavior.

## Retained bounds and behavior

The [B20 receive overread](B20.md) remains retail behavior: allocate/copy
size-1 but queue size. No short-message guard is added. Guest/startup dispatch
still assumes the relevant34/26-byte payload; ring fullness, six-player count
and mapping domains, receive-error continuation and stale data remain separate
B20/B42 contracts. The recovered signed fields preserve negative decoded
values too; they are not proof that peer-supplied values are valid.
