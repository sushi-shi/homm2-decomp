The first 20 physical PHILAI definitions, through `GetTurnAIVars`, now have
individual ten-pattern reviews. Both owner headers were read completely.
The source file is not fully reviewed; the later `HeroInteractionAtTown`

## B69: byte evidence and remaining contracts

`ResetHeroRVs` (`0x7e1ef`) passes the independent whole-function checker:
391 bytes, all DIR32 sites and complete ordered targets. Its final coordinate
store requires valid x/y beyond the explicit -1 guard. The nearby-hero
expression really uses the hero's x coordinate twice. At function offsets
`+0x137` and `+0x15a`, both `sub` instructions read
`[game + heroIndex*250 + 0x27dd]`, the hero x field. This is not corrected to y
as a reconstruction cleanup.

`DoAnywhereDDoorTownGate` (`0x7f72a`) also passes completely: 947 bytes and all
sites/targets. Candidate x/y are assigned before the visited lookup:

```text
function+0x241  load MAP_WIDTH
function+0x247  multiply by destY
function+0x24b  multiply by 9 (searchNode size)
function+0x252  multiply destX by 9
function+0x25a  load search storage pointer at +0x2414
function+0x262  read visited byte [nodes + 9*(destX+width*destY) + 4]
function+0x298  compare destX against zero
function+0x2ac  compare destY against zero
```

The coordinate guards do not protect that earlier read. An x=-1 candidate
on the top row is before the allocation; y=height is beyond its final row.
Other row-edge x candidates can alias a different row within the allocation.
The remaining selection predicates and normal map/event reachability have
not been proven for these counterexamples. No padding or reordered guard is
introduced to conceal or change the retail behavior.

`DoAI` (`0x7fd23`) has 2879 matching ordinary bytes and matching DIR32 sites.
The general checker leaves four private floating constants unresolved; this
is not claimed as independent whole-function closure. The relevant local
instruction bytes and named call targets do agree. The special-direction
branch at `+0x549` jumps via `+0xb2c` to `+0x84b`. It bypasses the normal path's
stores at `+0x79c` (path index), `+0x79f/+0x7a6` (move flags), and `+0x7ad`
(stop flag). At `+0x8a9`, the stop flag is loaded from `[ebp-4]` and passed to
`MoveHero`. On a first special move it has no earlier assignment; later moves
can inherit stale state. The flag output pointers alone do not prove that
their callees read uninitialized data. Determining ordinary reachability of
the special direction requires the still-pending target-selector review.

`GetTurnAIVars` (`0x808a1`) has 3295 matching ordinary bytes and matching DIR32
sites; eleven private float operands remain unresolved by that checker.
Its threat scan uses width for both grid dimensions. Its town branch also
forms the occupying hero and army pointers without checking the -1 occupant
sentinel: the byte at town+0x17 is sign-extended, multiplied by250, then added
to game+0x27c4 or game+0x2829. This is outside the hero array for -1, even if
the resulting address remains within the surrounding game object. Callee
behavior and normal threat-scan reachability remain to be reviewed.

Other per-function B69 entries record live owners, valid map/object IDs,
54 heroes, 72 towns, six players, eight owned-hero slots, path capacity256,
direction domains, and arithmetic/float representability. The mine-value
neighborhood is explicitly clipped and produces values0..4. `GoodAdjacent`
restores its two value factors on its ordinary tail, but not its stone-lith
early return; this observed state behavior is not a proposed bounds repair.

Evidence is in `build/ai-review-<rva>-{rva,xref,strings,diff,blocks,asm,retail}.log`.
The ordinary-byte/target checker has not been relaxed.
